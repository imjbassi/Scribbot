Build the robot’s turntable

One guide for the compact base you are printing. This replaces the earlier assembly instructions. It does not change any STL geometry.

1. What you are building

You are building the unpowered rotating platform at the bottom of the robot. The arm, wrist, pen holder and electronics are later stages. The small gear and servo will eventually turn this platform, but they stay off during this assembly.

Your current stopping point: the axle

You have a fully threaded M5 bolt. It can be a loose alignment aid for understanding the stack, if the parts slide on freely. It is not the approved bearing axle for a working base. Do not tighten this rehearsal assembly, attach the arm, or use a hand-turn result as proof of bearing fit.

The metal-sleeve idea mentioned earlier is not a designed replacement. No sleeve dimensions, replacement bearings or revised prints have been selected. Do not buy those parts on the basis of that suggestion.

Do this now

  1. Identify the printed parts using the table below.
  2. Separate the hardware you already have from the missing items.
  3. Use the diagram to understand the two stacks. A loose rehearsal is optional and only uses parts that fit without force.
  4. Resolve the axle and missing fasteners before the final fastening and rotation test.

No new print version is required by this guide. Keep the small gear and all accepted fit-test parts.

Why there are two stacks: the central bolt, pedestal and small spacers stay still. The larger printed shell turns. Each bearing joins the two: its inner ring stays still and its outer ring turns with the shell.

5. Assembly, one step at a time

These steps describe the intended completed stack. If hardware is missing, use them as a layout reference. With the threaded bolt you currently have, stop before final tightening and testing.

1

Lay out and identify the parts

Place #01–#09 on the table in numerical order. Put both #06 caps together. Keep the old bearing carrier separate: it has a recessed bearing pocket and is not a substitute for #03 or #05. Set the small motor gear, servo and servo plate aside.

Check: You have 10 printed pieces total, including two caps. No motor is connected.

2

Insert the central bolt through #01

Turn the base over. Insert the M5 bolt through the center from the underside so its head sits inside the round recess. Turn the base upright while holding the bolt in place. The long bolt now points up.

Check: The base rests flat and the bolt head does not protrude underneath. A different head shape or size may not fit.

3

Add #02 and the first narrow washer

Slide pedestal #02 over the bolt, broad end down on the base and narrow tip up. Place ONE narrow M5 washer on that tip. This is washer W1.

Check: The washer lies level on the tip. Do not substitute an M3 or M4 washer.

4

Add the lower cap, lower rotor and lower bearing

Slide one #06 cap over the center bolt and pedestal tip. Place #03 on the cap. Align their four corner holes. Slide a 625 bearing down the axle into the top of #03 until its inner metal ring rests on W1. Guide it gently; do not hammer it or press on the rubber seal.

Check: The bearing is inside #03. The cap below stops its outer ring from escaping downward; W1 supports its inner ring.

5

Build the spacer stack between bearings

On the lower bearing’s INNER metal ring, place W2, then spacer #08 (18 mm tall), then W3. All three slide over the central bolt.

Check: From the lower bearing upward: W2 → #08 → W3. The spacer does not sit on the outer ring or rubber seal.

6

Add the large gear and upper rotor

Lower #04 over the central stack until it rests on #03. Add #05 on top of the gear. Rotate the loose printed pieces as needed so all four corner holes line up. Nothing is driven by the servo yet.

Check: Outer printed order so far, bottom upward: #06 → #03 → #04 → #05.

7

Insert the upper bearing and close the shell

Slide the second 625 bearing down the central bolt into the top of #05 until its inner ring rests on W3. Add the second #06 cap, then platform #07. Align their four corner holes with the rest.

Check: There is one bearing near each end of the rotating shell, not two bearings together at one end.

8

Connect the outer shell with FOUR M3 × 45 screws

For each of the four corner holes: put a flat M3 washer under the screw head, insert the screw downward through #07, upper #06, #05, #04, #03 and lower #06, then add a flat M3 washer and M3 nut underneath. Tighten gradually in alternating corners, just until seated.

Check: These four screws connect the rotating shell ONLY. They do not go through #02 or #01. Your 30 mm screws are too short; do not substitute them.

9

Finish the central stack

Through the platform’s central opening, place W4 on the upper bearing’s inner ring, then #09 (10 mm), then W5. Add the first M5 nut above W5. For a loose rehearsal using your threaded bolt, STOP before tightening or testing.

Check: Central upper order: upper bearing → W4 → #09 → W5 → first M5 nut. These parts stay fixed while the platform turns around them.

10

Secure and test — ONLY with a suitable axle

With both bearings on a verified smooth shank and the specified hardware fitted, seat the lower M5 nut gently against the stationary stack. Add the second M5 nut. Hold the lower nut with one wrench while locking the second against it, so the lower nut does not tighten farther. Stop if anything binds.

Check: Hold #01 and turn #07 gently by hand. It should turn smoothly without scraping, tight spots or visible rocking. Repeat after locking the nuts. This is an unloaded mechanical check, not permission to connect power.

6. If something does not fit

SymptomWhat to check
Side screws do not reach the nutsThey must be M3 × 45, not the 30 mm screws in your kit. Check the two cap quantities and washer thickness. Do not omit washers or leave nuts partly engaged to make short screws fit.
A bearing will not enter the rotorConfirm it is a 625 bearing and you have #03/#05, not an older carrier. Check loose print strands. Do not hammer the bearing or scale the entire model.
Center hole is blocked by a spacerThe narrow central spacers are intended to occupy that opening. The rotating cap/platform must clear their outside surfaces.
Platform becomes tight when nuts are tightenedStop. Check washer contact, spacer order and whether the lower M5 nut moved while locking the second. Do not power the servo to overcome binding.
Platform rocks visiblyCheck the axle-to-bearing fit, outer-bearing fit and loose side bolts. A threaded axle is not an accepted final fit. Do not hide the play with glue or tape.
Base will not sit flatCheck bolt-head size, the underside recess and any loose bridge strands. Do not force the base flat with board screws.
Top M5 nuts will not seat correctlyVerify the bolt length, threaded region and actual washer/spacer heights. Do not shorten spacers just to expose threads.

7. What happens after this stage

  1. Resolve the axle and complete the unpowered turntable. It must rotate smoothly without visible rocking.
  2. Finish the motor connection. Your horn/gear alignment and servo-case clearance checks passed, but horn attachment screws and installed gear height remain unresolved. Keep the original servo-center screw; do not replace it with a longer generic screw.
  3. Set up and calibrate powered base travel. No wiring or powered-motion instructions are released in this guide.
  4. Build and test the shoulder, elbow, wrist and pen system. Full-arm geometry, collision checks, load tests and shoulder heating remain to be completed.
For now: identify and lay out the parts. A working assembly still needs the appropriate axle, four M3 × 45 screws, and the specified M5 nuts/washers. Do not order a sleeve or different bearings yet.
