# Stage 2: fit the MG90S beside the turntable

## Current status

You assembled the turntable with a fully threaded M5 axle and reported smooth turning and secured side bolts. Earlier wobble was reported; its cause and loaded behavior are not established. At your request, axle replacement is deferred for an UNLOADED prototype. Keep the arm off; inspect for renewed rocking, binding or wear before moving on to loaded work. This supersedes the earlier instruction to stop all fitting work pending axle replacement.

This stage positions the servo and checks gear height. It does not release powered operation. The horn-to-gear screws remain unspecified.

## Print only these two files

- posts_set_of_4.stl: print ONE copy of this file. It already contains all four 10 mm motor posts.
- shoes_set_of_4.stl: print ONE copy of this file. It already contains all four thin backing plates.

Use your tested PLA process, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.12 mm layers, 0.2 mm first layer, 100% scale, 4 walls and 100% infill; flat as exported, no supports. Do not print four copies of either file. The post height is provisional until the real installed horn height is checked.

Reuse your accepted MG90S mounting plate and small gear. Do not reprint the turntable. The existing plate is 64 x 44 mm; rotate it so its long direction runs front-to-back along the base's motor slots. Its raised label faces up.

## Hardware from your M3 kit

Four M3 x 30 BUTTON-head screws, eight M3 flat washers, four M3 nuts. Do not use M4 screws or split lock washers. The button heads are the low rounded heads in your first kit.

The backing plates bridge the wide slots underneath the base. Each is 10.4 x 18 x 1 mm, with a 3.4 mm hole. They belong inside the elongated underside recess, not on top of the base. Their narrow edge leaves limited adjustment; do not force them beyond the recess. This is a prototype printed support for the small motor mount, not a tested structural washer replacement for other joints.

## Install each of the four mount bolts

See motor_mount_guide.png. Start beneath the base:

M3 screw head -> M3 flat washer -> thin printed backing plate -> slotted base -> 10 mm post -> existing MG90S plate -> M3 flat washer -> M3 nut.

1. Lift/support the base to reach its underside; do not dismantle the central turntable.
2. Put a washer on each M3 x 30 button-head screw. Pass it through a backing plate, then UP through a motor slot from below. The backing plate's 18 mm direction follows the long recess.
3. Put one 10 mm post over each screw on TOP of the base.
4. Lower the existing servo plate onto all four posts. Add washers and nuts above it. Leave the nuts loose enough for gentle adjustment.
5. Check underneath. Each backing plate must lie flat in its recess and bridge the slot. Screw heads must not make the base rock on the table. If they protrude or a backing plate bends, stop and report that; do not crush it by tightening.

## Place the servo and check gear height

6. With its horn removed, gently feed the MG90S lead and body through the plate's rectangular opening from ABOVE. Mounting tabs rest on the plate. Do not trap the cable between the plate, posts or base. If the cable plug will not pass, route it before lowering the plate.
7. The servo shaft faces UP. Start with the output shaft approximately aligned with the middle of the large gear in the front-to-back direction; the adjustment slots compensate for the actual servo shaft offset. Keep the small gear out of mesh for now.
8. Servo-tab fastening is not resolved by the M3 kit: the plate has 2.2 mm mounting holes. Do not force M3 screws into them. You may hold the servo seated by hand for this static alignment check. This is not a secure operating mount.
9. Seat the already-tested white horn on the shaft without forcing or twisting it. Hold your small gear on top in its confirmed flush orientation. It is not secured yet; keep a hand on it.
10. Look from the side. The two gears are each 6 mm thick. Their top and bottom faces should be at approximately the same heights, with most of their tooth height overlapping. If the small gear is high or low, do not tilt the servo plate: all four posts must change height equally. The 10 mm posts are a starting point, not a measured final height.
11. Move the plate gently toward the large gear until teeth can enter the gaps. If tooth tips meet, move apart and rotate the LARGE platform slightly, then bring them together. Do not force the small gear or back-drive the servo. The plate should remain level; neither gear nor horn should rub the servo case or platform.
12. Take a SIDE-ON photo showing both gears and the servo plate. Keep the small gear loose and disengage it after the check. Do not rotate the whole assembly as a driven system until the gear is fastened and servo travel is calibrated.

## Report these three things

- Does the servo plate sit level, with the base still flat on the table?
- Is the small gear level with the large gear, higher, or lower?
- Does the cable have clearance without being pinched?

A side-on photo can answer most of this. Include the supplied servo screws beside the assembly if handy; that helps distinguish the original center screw from the mounting screws without assuming their sizes.

## Before powered use (not completed in this stage)

Select and verify servo-tab fasteners and two opposed horn-to-gear fasteners, resolve actual gear height and mesh clearance, secure the base, and calibrate the servo with the gears disengaged. The original center screw attaches the horn to the servo; it does NOT secure the printed gear to the horn. Do not glue the gear or substitute a longer center screw.
